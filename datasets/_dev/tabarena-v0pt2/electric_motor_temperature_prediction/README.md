## In brief

**Question.** How hot are the permanent magnets in an electric motor's rotor right now, given the readings a vehicle can measure?

**Use case.** The motor control of an electric vehicle estimates the rotor magnet temperature in real time, as it is not reliably and economically measurable in a commercial vehicle, to use the motor fully without overheating it.

**One row.** One half-second sample of one of 69 test-bench sessions of a prototype permanent magnet synchronous motor at Paderborn University (1,296,316 samples).

**Predicted.** The permanent magnet temperature at that sample, in degrees Celsius.

**Known at prediction time.** The motor's voltages, currents and speed and the coolant and ambient temperatures at that sample, quantities derived from them (power, magnitudes), and their running averages and spreads over the session so far.

**What the test data stands for.** Whole sessions are held out, so the test data stands for driving cycles not seen in training, on the same motor.

**Our changes to the source.** We drop the stator temperatures and the torque, as the study does, add the study's derived inputs and their running averages over four window lengths, and drop each session's first 500 samples as warm-up.

> **Use case fit: direct (the rows, features and split mirror the use case).** The inputs are sensor readings a vehicle has, smoothed over the past only, and a held-out session is a new driving cycle. The task covers one prototype motor on a test bench.

## Curation notes

We select the task to predict permanent magnet temperature from the input features. We create grouped splits on the profile_id, which corresponds to predicting the temperature for an unseen motor run session, thus simulating how the model would be used in reality.

- The original study also incorporates temporal connection within the session. We re-create a time-index (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/wkirgsn/electric-motor-temperature/discussion/147446).
- We also add the derived inputs used in the original study.
- We also add the EWMA/WEMS features with window sizes of [500, 2000, 4000, 8000]. To avoid the problem of having an incorrect EWMA/WEMS state and since not all recording are warmed up (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/wkirgsn/electric-motor-temperature/discussion/117319), we drop the first 500 samples (first span) of each profile to simulate such a warm-up.
- We take the full data (about 1.30M rows after the warm-up drop) with grouped 3-fold cross-validation over the 69 profiles, so every fold trains on about 860k rows. Until 2026-10 an `electric_motor_temperature_prediction_1m` version sub-sampled it to one 1M / 250k split.
- Time basis: Timeless: 69 bench sessions of designed driving cycles of one prototype motor; the magnet
  temperature is the motor's physical response to the session's inputs, carried by smoothed features of the
  session's own past; whole sessions are held out. The sessions have no dates, so an ageing of the motor between
  sessions cannot be checked. Not tested: another motor.

## Group structure

One group is a measurement session (profile) of the test bench; its rows are its samples in time order. The use case is a real-time estimate of motor temperatures that "are not reliably and economically measurable in a commercial vehicle" (Kaggle; Kirchgässner et al.), so each sample is one prediction, made from its own row; its smoothed features already use only the session's past inputs. The authors hold out whole profiles.
