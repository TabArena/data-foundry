## In brief

**Question.** How will a cryptocurrency's price move over the next 15 minutes, compared with the market?

**Use case.** At the close of each minute, a trading firm forecasts each coin's return over the next 15 minutes relative to the market, to decide its positions (the G-Research competition).

**One row.** One coin at one minute: 14 cryptocurrencies, January 2018 to September 2021.

**Predicted.** The coin's return from one to 16 minutes ahead, minus the part that follows the market's return (a weighted mean of the 14 coins); values sit close to zero.

**Known at prediction time.** The coin and the minute, the minute's trading summary (open, high, low and close prices, volume, average price, number of trades) and the price trends of the past hour, 5 hours and 15 hours, for the coin and for the market.

**What the test data stands for.** Each test window is 91 days at the end of the period, newest first; training is about a million earlier coin-minute rows, drawn as whole minutes (all coins of a minute) from the whole history, with a 15-minute gap.

**Our changes to the source.** We add the price-trend features of the third-place solution, as the minute itself carries no signal, and drop the minutes without a target.

> **Use case fit: direct (the rows, features and split mirror the use case).** Everything the model sees is known at the minute and the target covers the next 15 minutes, as in trading. Under the competition's asset-weighted Pearson correlation, a 12-seed ensemble of early-stopped LightGBM (without the timestamp) scores +0.039, +0.033 and +0.021 in the three windows, leads the coin's mean in all three, clearly in the newer two, and is positive on 75-77 of 91 days; R² stays within ±0.003.

## Curation notes

We start with train.csv (one row per asset and minute, 14 cryptocurrencies, 2018-01-01 to 2021-09-21) and asset_details.csv of the Kaggle competition.

- Prediction point: at the close of minute t we predict an asset's `Target`, its return from minute t+1 to t+16 minus its beta times the weighted mean return of the 14 assets (the organisers' tutorial notebook). Known then: every one-minute bar of the 14 assets up to and including minute t.
- Scope: the target is the asset's own residualised forward return, and its signal is the asset's trailing return relative to the 14-asset mean. The bar alone, the asset's own lags alone and the market means alone carry none (LightGBM on each set, three test windows). The curator keeps it as a forecasting-like panel under the amended TP-10 (Lennart, 2026-10-22). Leg 1, measured by the declared metric: early-stopped LightGBM scores weighted Pearson +0.040, +0.052 and +0.045 in the three windows (95% day-block intervals [+0.023, +0.063], [+0.036, +0.070], [+0.027, +0.063]), and its lead over the asset's mean (the naive forecast: -0.002, +0.007, +0.008) excludes 0 in every window (random-row train sides, 1-day gap; superseded 2026-11-06: on the shipped splits, whole-minute train sides and a 15-minute gap, the 12-seed ensemble, which averages the 12 models' predictions and leaves out `timestamp`, scores +0.0388 [+0.0205, +0.0623], +0.0333 [+0.0103, +0.0585] and +0.0206 [-0.0050, +0.0496] (a single model +0.0368, +0.0294 and +0.0199), its lead over the coin's mean +0.0384 [+0.0182, +0.0631], +0.0418 [+0.0138, +0.0699] and +0.0231 [-0.0060, +0.0537], +0.034 pooled over the three windows (SE about 0.008), positive on 75, 75 and 77 of 91 days: the oldest window's lead no longer excludes 0). Leg 1 still holds (CC 2026-11-08, Lennart; REVIEW.md issue 8): the model is ahead of the coin's mean in every window by its point estimate, and the interval is read pooled, as for ashrae_great_energy_predictor_iii and rain_in_australia. Leg 2: the top three solutions are LightGBM on point-in-time features (G-Research's wrap-up), and no feature is a lag of the `Target` column; the relative returns are built from Close and correlate 0.80-0.83 (median over the 14 assets) with sums of the asset's lagged `Target`.
- History features (`history_features`): a row's own bar carries no signal for the next 15 minutes (a LightGBM prediction from the bar correlates -0.002, 0.005 and 0.010 with the target in the three test windows), so we add the features of the 3rd-place solution: the public notebook "Training 3rd place solution" (sugghi), which Kaggle's write-up record for the 3rd place lists as sugghi's (sole author, team GABA); it uses Close only and trains from 12 January 2019. For 60, 300 and 900 minutes: the log of Close over its trailing mean and the log return, their mean over the assets (`market_*`) and the asset's difference from it (`*_minus_market`). With them the correlation is 0.026, 0.059 and 0.049.
- The 18 features stay after a comparison with the other top solutions (CLEAN-19; Lennart, 2026-10-22): the 2nd place withholds its features (LightGBM on squared loss) and the 1st place has no write-up; lower places add a 15-minute look-back, volume and volatility, technical indicators, the hour, and other coins' returns. Added to the 18 (LightGBM, mean of 2 seeds, change in weighted Pearson in the three windows; random-row train sides, 1-day gap, 2026-10-22): a 15-minute look-back -0.001, -0.001, -0.002; volume and volatility +0.003, +0.004, -0.005; other coins' 15- and 60-minute returns -0.005, -0.013, -0.007; bar shape and time -0.007, -0.007, -0.004. None helps in all three windows.
- We compute the features on the full minute grid before dropping rows. A missing minute takes the asset's last Close for at most 100 minutes (as in the notebook). A trailing mean reaching into a longer gap or before the asset's first minute is missing; a log return checks only its end point, so 193, 1,804 and 9,370 values (60, 300 and 900 minutes) span a longer gap, as in the notebook.
- We drop the 750,338 rows (3.1%) without a target: Close(t+1) or Close(t+16) is missing (37% lack only Close(t+1)), and the competition does not score them. Their bars still enter the history features.
- We replace `Asset_ID` by the asset's name from asset_details.csv (`Asset_Name`, category); its `Weight` in asset_details.csv, the asset's weight in the metric, ships as a scoring column and is not a feature.
- `VWAP` is a positive price and `Volume` a traded amount: we set the infinite and negative VWAP values (13 rows of the frame, 15 of the raw file, all Maker, June 2020) and the negative Volume values (2 rows of the frame, 3 raw, Maker) to missing.
- We convert `timestamp` (Unix seconds) to a datetime.
- Anomaly: 16 bars of the frame (18 raw) have a `Low` below half of their open and close and 3 raw bars a `High` above twice, and 266 targets exceed 0.2 in absolute value (mostly Bitcoin Cash around its November 2018 fork). We keep them as unusual but real. No (timestamp, asset) pair repeats.
- Temporal, not grouped by asset: the competition forecasts the same 14 assets on later data; a split by asset would only test a shift between 14 assets (decision table).
- History: each train side is all earlier history back to 2018, sampled to the 1M budget as whole minutes (every asset of a drawn minute kept, `sample_by="time"`); SPLIT-20 reason (Lennart, 2026-10-22): the target and the features are relative to the 14-asset market at each minute, and random rows left about 1.3 assets per minute. Measured at the 1M budget (weighted Pearson, mean of 3 seeds, windows 1, 2, 3; on 2026-10-22, on the test rows then shipped, drawn as random rows, with a 1-day gap): whole minutes over all history .041, .046, .045; random rows over all history .042, .045, .043; the newest 1M rows (50 days) .036, .045, .007. A recent cut swings by window and collapses in the third; the 2nd place found that longer history kept helping and trained on all of it. The test windows from 2020-12-22 to 2021-06-21 fall in the 2021 high-volatility regime (median absolute 900-minute return 0.029-0.036, against 0.013-0.022 in every quarter of 2019-2020).
- Metric: the competition's weighted Pearson correlation, each row weighted by its asset's `Weight` (Bitcoin 6.78 ... IOTA and Maker 1.10). It replaces the pooled `pearsonr` declared before as its stand-in, which ranked seven predictions per window as the weighted correlation does (Kendall tau 1.0).
- Training weight (MET-15, 2026-11-06): none. `Weight` is a function of `Asset_Name`, which the model sees, so MET-15 needs no sample weight: a weight that is a function of the features leaves the best squared-error prediction of each row unchanged. Fitting with it as the sample weight (and in the validation loss) moved the weighted Pearson by +0.0045, +0.0034 and +0.0001 in the three windows (mean of 12 seeds; better in 25 of 36 window-seed pairs; +0.0010 with a fixed 50 rounds instead of early stopping, every interval including 0), within the spread across windows (mean +0.0026, SD 0.0023, t about 1.96 on 2 df); only the newest window's interval excludes 0 (12-seed ensembles, +0.0050 [+0.0005, +0.0099], 95% day-block interval).
- Note: the task probes flag no_signal, drift_baseline and unstable (untuned models, scored by R^2; by the declared weighted Pearson their LightGBM scores 0.028, the linear model 0.023 and the dummy 0, mean of the 3 splits). LightGBM (learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, at least 200 rows per leaf) early-stopped on the newest 10% of each train side reaches R^2 +0.0008, +0.0008 and -0.0023 on the shipped splits (mean of 4 seeds; their ensemble +0.0010 [-0.0002, +0.0026], +0.0009 [+0.0001, +0.0017] and -0.0019 [-0.0046, +0.0011], 95% day-block intervals, only the middle window's excluding 0). Under the declared weighted Pearson the same signal is clear in the two newer windows and pooled over the three (Scope bullet); drift_baseline comes from a target centred on zero.

Version of `g_research_crypto_forecasting`: We sample per test window (v2 split protocol) by whole minutes: each window keeps at most 500k of its rows and its train side at most 1M of all earlier rows, drawn as whole minutes (every asset of a drawn minute kept; one random order of the minutes for all windows); the frame keeps only the rows a split uses. The full frame has 23,486,468 rows (14 assets, one row per minute, 2018-01-01 to 2021-09-20). The train sides cover the whole history.

## Splits

The competition scored the submitted models on the 3 months of live data after its submission deadline. We use each of the last 3 windows of 91 days (newest first) as a test window and all earlier data as train data, with a 15-minute gap, the minimum: a row's target needs the Close 16 minutes later, so the newest training label is known at the close of the window's first minute, when the first test prediction is made (Lennart, 2026-10-22; against a 1-day gap the weighted Pearson moves by -0.0003 on average, inside the seed noise). Each window keeps at most 500k of its rows and each train side at most 1M of all earlier rows, both drawn as whole minutes (every asset of a drawn minute kept; one random order of the minutes for all windows), as the target and the features are relative to the 14-asset market at each minute. Rows of one asset a few minutes apart share most of their 15-minute target window (per asset, targets 5 minutes apart correlate with a median of 0.57, range 0.25-0.66), so validation inside a train side has to split by time blocks, not rows: LightGBM early-stopped on random rows of the train side stops after 220-528 rounds and scores test R^2 -0.0049, -0.0043 and -0.0090, against +0.0006, +0.0013 and +0.0004 when it stops on the newest 10% (random-row train sides, 1-day gap).

3 repeat(s) x 1 fold(s) with seed 4267; train 999,991 to 1,000,000 rows, test 499,992 to 500,000 rows. Prediction horizon: 91 days. Gap between train and test: 15 minutes. Row budget: at most 1M train / 500k test rows per split.
