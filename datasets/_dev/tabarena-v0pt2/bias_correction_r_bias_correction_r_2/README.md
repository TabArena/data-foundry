## In brief

**Question.** What will tomorrow's lowest air temperature be at a Seoul weather station?

**Use case.** Each evening, once the Korea Meteorological Administration's regional model run of 9 pm is out, a forecaster corrects the model's forecast of the next day's minimum temperature for each station's local bias (Cho et al. 2020).

**One row.** One station on one summer day: 25 automatic weather stations in Seoul, June 30 to August 30 of each year 2013-2017.

**Predicted.** The station's minimum air temperature on the next day, in degrees Celsius.

**Known at prediction time.** The station with its location, elevation and slope, the day's incoming solar radiation, the highest and lowest temperatures observed that day up to 9 pm, and the model's forecasts for the next day (temperature, humidity, wind, latent heat, cloud cover, rain).

**What the test data stands for.** Nine test weeks cover the last summer (2017-06-30 to 2017-08-30); each trains on every earlier station-day up to one day before the week, as for a correction refit weekly.

**Our changes to the source.** We keep one of the two targets, the next day's minimum, and drop the maximum, observed after the prediction point; we split by date instead of at random, as the stations of one day share its weather.

> **Use case fit: direct (the rows, features and split mirror the use case).** Everything the model sees is out by the evening, and each test week follows its training days as in operation. The task is narrow: 25 stations of one city, summers only.

## Curation notes

We start with Bias_correction_ucl.csv from UCI (dataset 514): 7,750 station-days of 25 automatic weather
stations (AWS) of the Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) in Seoul, June 30 to August 30 of each year
2013-2017. Each row holds 14 next-day forecasts of KMA's LDAPS model, the day's observed extremes and two
targets, the next day's minimum (`Next_Tmin`) and maximum (`Next_Tmax`) air temperature.

- Prediction point: the evening of the present day (`Date`), once the LDAPS run of 21 KST is out; the
  present-day extremes cover 0-21 KST (Cho et al. 2020, Sec. 2.2-2.3). The model corrects the bias of the LDAPS
  forecast of the next day's minimum at the station.
- One target, `Next_Tmin`: the v2 task holds one target. For the minimum, the mean of untuned CatBoost, LightGBM
  and ridge beats the standard correction, model output statistics (MOS) fitted per station, on 7 of 9 windows;
  for the maximum, MOS beats every untuned model (decision table). We drop `Next_Tmax`: it is observed on the
  next day, after the prediction point.
- Temporal split, not random: the 25 stations of a day share its weather, and only 17.5% of the variance of
  `Next_Tmin` lies within a day: untuned LightGBM reaches RMSE 0.62 with random folds and 1.00 when each day's
  stations stay on one side (decision table). The paper also validates by hindcast on all earlier days (Sec.
  3.3).
- `station` stays a feature (category): the same 25 stations report every day, and the model serves them on
  later days. The paper's leave-one-station-out test (Sec. 4.3) asks a secondary question.
- We drop the last two rows of the file: they have no station and no date and hold the minimum and the maximum
  of every column.
- We drop the 27 rows without a target (both targets are missing together).
- We drop the 75 station-days without an LDAPS run (2013-08-10, 2016-07-31 and 2016-08-20, all in train): the
  task corrects the forecast, and without one there is nothing to correct. The observed extremes missing on 60
  other station-days stay missing: a station can fail on the evening of a prediction.
- Note: the targets are the AWS daily extremes, the present-day columns the extremes of hourly readings up to 21
  KST (Sec. 2.3). So a row's label is close to, and mostly below, the next day's `Present_Tmin` at the station,
  a value known only on the next day (decision table).
- Note: the task probes flag no_spread and unstable; we keep the task. Untuned LightGBM beats the training mean
  on 8 of 9 windows (skill 0.61-0.96); in the oldest, 2017-06-30 to 07-06, the training mean is already within
  RMSE 1.11 and LightGBM falls just below it (skill -0.07). The skill varies with the training mean's error
  between weeks, and a window of 6-7 days separates strong methods little. Once tuned results exist, check
  that tuned single models beat the per-station MOS.
- Anomaly: UCI and the paper's Table 1 give `LDAPS_CC1`-`LDAPS_CC4` (cloud cover) and `LDAPS_PPT1`-`LDAPS_PPT4`
  (precipitation) in "%", but the cloud covers lie between 0 and 0.98. We keep the values as published.
- Note: the paper scores with R^2, bias and RMSE (Sec. 3.3); we keep the default RMSE.

## Splits

Weather models are refit often (one to two weeks at most, as the curators argue for
climate_model_weather_forecasting), so a bias-correction model is refit and then used every evening for 7 days.
9 test windows of 7 days walk back from 2017-08-30, newest first, and cover the whole last summer (2017-06-30 to
2017-08-30; the oldest window holds 6 days). Train is every earlier station-day from 2013-06-30 up to a gap of 1
day: the label of the day before a window is the minimum of the window's first day, which is complete only at
midnight, after the first prediction that evening. Each window holds 149-175 station-days (6-7 days of the 25
stations), and the oldest trains on the four summers 2013-2016. The 9 windows are the 3 x 3 splits of the v2
protocol for this size; the paper's own hindcast refits every day (Cho et al. 2020, Sec. 3.3).

## Decisions

### 1. Temporal split: the 25 stations of a day share its weather

Most of the target's variance lies between days, so a random split would train on the other stations of each test day. Untuned LightGBM on 3 folds: RMSE 0.62 with random folds, 1.00 with each day's stations on one side.

| statistic | Next_Tmin | Next_Tmax |
|---|---|---|
| standard deviation | 2.48 | 3.12 |
| mean within-day standard deviation (25 stations) | 1.02 | 0.93 |
| standard deviation of the daily means | 2.26 | 2.99 |
| share of the variance within days | 0.175 | 0.088 |

### 2. Next_Tmin is the target; Next_Tmax is dropped

The v2 task holds one target. For the minimum, the mean of untuned CatBoost, LightGBM and ridge (RMSE 0.785) beats the best per-station MOS (0.823) on 7 of the 9 windows; for the maximum, MOS on the forecast alone (1.575) beats every untuned model (1.587-1.676) and ties their mean (1.578). The maximum is observed on the next day, after the prediction point, so it cannot stay as a feature.

| LDAPS forecast | Next_Tmin | Next_Tmax |
|---|---|---|
| bias of the raw forecast, 2013-2017 | 0.6 | -0.62 |
| RMSE of the raw forecast, 2013-2017 | 1.3 | 1.85 |
| mean RMSE, 9 windows: raw forecast | 1.202 | 1.843 |
| mean RMSE, 9 windows: forecast minus the station's mean error | 0.87 | 1.618 |
| mean RMSE, 9 windows: per-station MOS on the forecast | 0.855 | 1.575 |
| mean RMSE, 9 windows: per-station MOS on forecast and present extreme | 0.823 | 1.625 |

### 3. The targets are AWS daily extremes, the present-day columns hourly extremes up to 21 KST

A row's target is not the next row's present-day value: the daily minimum lies below the lowest hourly reading and the daily maximum above the highest one (Cho et al. 2020, Sec. 2.3).

| same station, next day | pairs | above | equal | below | mean (degrees C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Next_Tmin - next day's Present_Tmin | 7564 | 0.0% | 17.7% | 82.3% | -0.25 |
| Next_Tmax - next day's Present_Tmax | 7564 | 94.1% | 5.7% | 0.2% | 0.55 |

### 4. Nine one-week test windows over the last summer, with a one-day gap

One- or two-week windows follow what the curators argue for weather (climate_model_weather_forecasting). Walking back from the newest data, fourteen-day windows stop after five, at the empty weeks before July 2017. Explicit cutoffs in the summers 2016 and 2017 give 9 fourteen-day windows with 118 test days, but they separate the methods no better: paired standard errors between untuned CatBoost, LightGBM and ridge of 0.023-0.047 RMSE, against 0.028-0.042 on the shipped windows.

| option | windows | first test day | test days | test rows | train share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 days x 9, gap 1 day (shipped) | 9 | 2017-06-30 | 62 | 149-175 | 80%-97% |
| 7 days x 9, no gap | 9 | 2017-06-30 | 62 | 149-175 | 80%-98% |
| 14 days x 9 walking back, gap 1 day | 5 | 2017-06-30 | 62 | 149-349 | 80%-95% |
| 14 days x 9, cutoffs in 2016 and 2017, gap 1 day | 9 | 2016-07-06 | 116 | 149-350 | 62%-95% |
