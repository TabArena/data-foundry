## In brief

**Question.** Will a customer who takes a consumer loan turn out to be a bad customer?

**Use case.** The source states no use case; it presents the data as an example of imbalanced classes. For example, a lender decides when an application arrives whether to grant a loan for a phone, an appliance or a service.

**One row.** One consumer loan, with the kind of product or service it pays for (1,723 loans; the lender, country and period are not stated).

**Predicted.** Whether the customer is marked a bad client (11% are); what makes a client bad, and by when, is not stated.

**Known at prediction time.** The loan (amount, term, a month number, the kind of product bought) and the customer (age, sex, education, income, family status, children, region, phone operator, and whether already a client).

**What the test data stands for.** Random loans, so the test data stands for new customers of the same lender and period.

> **Use case fit: approximate (a stand-in with a known gap).** The features are what an application holds, but the label has no stated definition or horizon, and the source names no lender, place or period.

## Curation notes

- We renamed the values of the target variable to be more descriptive.
- Note: The original source of the data is still to be clarified (open item in the curation record).
- Ordinal scales (2026-10-30, the curator's marks of 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01, CLEAN-14): 1 column is ordered
  categories in the order of the source's codebook (`ORDINAL_LEVELS`).
- Cycle (2026-11-01, CLEAN-14, Lennart's rule): `month` (1-12) stays a number. A weekday or a month is a
  position in a continuous cycle (weekday 1.5 is Monday noon), and the source ships it as a number; named
  weekdays and months are ordered categories instead.
- Checked for generated data (2026-11-10, kept by Andrej, 2026-11-11): none of the marks of a generator
  (no rules on the row number, no repeated rows, structural zeros only of lending policy, complete integer ranges),
  and 9 of 13 columns are tied to the rest of the row, as in real credit data. One oddity: `family_status` is
  independent of every other column, age included (shuffle-test ROC AUC 0.488; 27% married at 18 to 22 and at over
  60; unrelated to children, p = 0.9), so it reads as scrambled or meaningless. It does not separate the classes
  (p = 0.1) and is kept as recorded.
- Time basis: StationaryRelation (Andrej, 2026-11-20): loans of twelve consecutive months; the uploader's file is in month order (`month` 1 to 12 once, rank correlation with the row 0.996), and over the months the bad rate stays flat (6.2% to 15.4%, chi-squared p = 0.35) and five monthly windows, each trained on all earlier months, score as random splits of the same sizes (ROC AUC 0.737 against 0.728, untuned LightGBM; 0.739 against 0.731, logistic regression). A split by month could not wait for the labels: the bad mark comes after a delay the source does not state, about a loan term. Not tested: loans of a later year or another credit climate, as the file covers twelve months of one lender.
