## In brief

**Question.** Will a credit card customer fail to pay within 18 months of the latest monthly statement?

**Use case.** At a customer's latest monthly statement, the card issuer estimates whether the customer will default in the next 18 months, to manage its lending risk, for example the credit it extends.

**One row.** One monthly statement of an American Express credit card customer; a customer has 1 to 13 statements, and the prediction is made once per customer, at the latest one (458,913 customers).

**Predicted.** Whether the customer defaults on a payment in the 18 months after the latest statement. The source kept only 5% of the customers who paid, so defaulters' statements are 24.9% of the rows.

**Known at prediction time.** All of the customer's statements up to the latest, with their dates, each with anonymized, normalized variables that the source groups into delinquency, spending, payments, balances and risk.

**What the test data stands for.** Whole customers are held out, so the test data stands for other customers of the same months, each scored at the latest statement.

> **Use case fit: direct (the rows, features and split mirror the use case).** Every statement is in the past at the prediction point, and the label covers a fixed 18 months after it, as in use. The test customers come from the same months as the training ones, and the features are anonymized.

## Curation notes

We start with the raw data from Kaggle and do not apply any further preprocessing to simulate a pipeline that can handle raw non-IID grouped data.
- Missing values: raddar's integer re-encoding writes a missing value as -1. In the 55 integer columns that hold
  -1 (int8 and int16, among them the categorical D_64, D_66, D_68, B_30, B_38, D_114, D_116, D_117, D_120 and
  D_126), -1 is the minimum, no cell is missing, and the -1 cells fall on the same rows across blocks (the same
  176,716 rows in D_113 to D_125, 101,548 in D_103, D_107, D_129, D_139, D_143 and D_145, 2,016 in B_16 to
  B_38). `_clean` sets them back to missing (CLEAN-10, 2026-10-30).
- The target (2026-11-01, CLEAN-25): `target` ships its classes as words (1 is the event). Binary: "Yes" is the
  event, the class that sorts second, so it is the metrics' positive class.
- The row budget (1M train, 500k test rows): We keep the whole frame (5,531,451 statements of 458,913 customers) and use grouped 3-fold cross-validation over it (v2 split protocol). Each fold tests on at most 500k rows, whole customers drawn from its third and stratified on the target, and trains on every other row (about 5.03M). The train budget is recorded in the container, not applied: a benchmark sub-samples each train side to 1M rows with `subsample_train`, whose default draws whole customers at random, stratified on the target.
- Time basis: UnseenGroup (Andrej, 2026-11-18; `ForcedGroupedFromTemporal` from 2026-11-16, `DelayedTarget`
  before): the use case scores customers the data does not hold, so whole customers are held out, each with
  its statements. Every customer is scored at its latest statement, and all 458,913 latest statements fall in
  March 2018; the label covers a fixed 18 months after it. Not tested: a later scoring month, which later
  scoring months in a release would allow.

## Group structure

One group is a customer; its rows are its monthly statements (1 to 13). The task is "to predict, for each customer_ID, the probability of a future payment default", with the label "calculated by observing 18 months performance window after the latest credit card statement" (AmEx Default Prediction, Kaggle). So one prediction per customer, made at its latest statement (`S_2`) from all its statements, which are all in the past at that point; the Kaggle test customers are disjoint from the training customers.
