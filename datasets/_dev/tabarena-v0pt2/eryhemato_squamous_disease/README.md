## In brief

**Question.** Which of six scaly red skin diseases does a patient have, judged from the clinical examination?

**Use case.** A dermatologist examines a patient with red, scaly skin and makes a differential diagnosis among six similar diseases before taking a biopsy, the first of the source's two steps.

**One row.** One patient diagnosed with one of the six diseases at the Department of Dermatology, Gazi University, Ankara (366 patients, published 1998; period not stated).

**Predicted.** The diagnosis: psoriasis (31%), seborrheic dermatitis, lichen planus, pityriasis rosea, chronic dermatitis or pityriasis rubra pilaris (5%).

**Known at prediction time.** Ten clinical findings graded 0 to 3 (redness, scaling, definite borders, itching, the Koebner phenomenon, polygonal and follicular papules, and involvement of the mouth, knees and elbows, and scalp), family history and age.

**What the test data stands for.** Random patients.

**Our changes to the source.** We drop the 22 findings from the biopsy and keep the clinical examination; with the biopsy the task is close to solved.

> **Use case fit: approximate (a stand-in with a known gap).** The clinical findings come before the biopsy and the label is the confirmed diagnosis, so the task is the differential the source describes. But the patients look like typical cases rather than a clinic's: 93% of those with lichen planus have mouth lesions and no other patient does, against 19% to 63% in clinic series. It is small and old.

## Curation notes

We start with the data from UCI.

- We keep only the clinical features: the 10 clinical findings graded 0 to 3, family history and age. The source describes two steps: "Patients were first evaluated clinically with 12 features. Afterwards, skin samples were taken for the evaluation of 22 histopathological features." With the 22 histopathological features the task is close to solved (a logistic model reaches macro ROC AUC 0.999 and 97% accuracy on the shipped splits); the task here is the clinical differential before the biopsy (macro ROC AUC 0.98, 87% accuracy), where seborrheic dermatitis, pityriasis rosea and chronic dermatitis are the hard cases.
- `family history` (0/1) is a category; age and the 10 graded findings are numbers (below).
- We ensure missing values in age are encoded as NaN.
- Note: the patients look selected. 93% of those with lichen planus have mouth lesions, against 56% of 91 biopsy-confirmed patients in another clinic's series (Samreen A, Luqman N, Asgher R, Khalid M, Shaheen JA. Frequency of oral involvement in cutaneous lichen planus patients. J Pak Assoc Dermatol. 2021;31(3):454-458. doi:10.66344/jpad.v31i3.1817). All 20 with pityriasis rubra pilaris are aged 7 to 22, though the disease is most common in adults; the source calls it "a children's disease".
- Note: one psoriasis patient has age 0, the only patient younger than 7. It may be an infant or a stand-in for an unknown age (the source marks 8 unknown ages with "?"); we keep it.
- Codes decoded (2026-11-01, CLEAN-12, CLEAN-14; the curator's rule: a code whose codebook says in words what
  each level is carries the words, a score without them is a number): 10 scores with no words per level are
  numbers (`SCORES`): `erythema`, `scaling`, `definite borders`, `itching`, `koebner phenomenon`, `polygonal
  papules`, `follicular papules`, `oral mucosal involvement`, `knee and elbow involvement`, `scalp involvement`.
- The target (2026-11-01, CLEAN-25): `class` ships its classes as words (dermatology.names, 9. Class
  Distribution).
- The 10 scores ship as numbers (2026-11-05, Lennart, GitHub issue #48, CLEAN-14): `_feature_types` had cast
  every column but age to a category, so they shipped unordered although the decoding above made them
  numbers. The source names only the ends of the scale ("0 indicates that the feature was not present, 3
  indicates the largest amount possible, and 1, 2 indicate the relative intermediate values").
- Time basis: ForcedIIDFromTemporal: patients examined over a period the source does not state (published
  1998); the file keeps no date but has two parts, rows 1 to 169 in mixed order and rows 170 to 366 mostly in
  runs of one diagnosis. The clinical findings tell them apart (random forest ROC AUC 0.70; 0.54 from the
  diagnosis alone). The random split puts both parts on both sides, so each test patient has training patients
  of its own part: on the shipped splits, logistic regression's log loss on one part's patients is 0.536 and
  0.345 when it trains on a random mix of both parts, against 0.688 and 0.425 when it trains on as many
  patients of the other part alone; told the part, it improves by another 0.014, and the case mix of the parts
  adds nothing (Slurm job 1373758). Read as two collection periods, too few for three windows; dates of
  examination would allow a split by period.
