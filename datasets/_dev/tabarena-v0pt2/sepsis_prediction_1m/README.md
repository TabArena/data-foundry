## In brief

**Question.** Will an intensive-care patient develop sepsis within the next six hours?

**Use case.** Every hour of an intensive-care stay, an early-warning system scores the patient's risk of sepsis in the next six hours, so the care team can start treatment early (PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019).

**One row.** One hour of one intensive-care stay at one of two US hospitals: the 1,552,210 hours of the 40,336 stays in the challenge's public data.

**Predicted.** Whether sepsis begins within six hours or has already begun: the label turns on six hours before onset and stays on.

**Known at prediction time.** That hour's measurements only: vital signs, lab values (most missing in a given hour), age, sex, the care unit, the time since hospital admission, the hours since intensive-care admission, and the hospital.

**What the test data stands for.** Whole stays are held out in three folds, so the test data stands for new patients of the same two hospitals; each patient is scored over its ordered hours.

**Our changes to the source.** Each hour is predicted from its own row only, where the challenge allowed the earlier hours, and the challenge's hidden third hospital is not available, so no shift to a new hospital is tested.

> **Use case fit: direct (the rows, features and split mirror the use case).** Each hour's measurements are known at that hour and the label looks six hours ahead, as for an early-warning system; seeing only the current hour is stricter than the challenge. New patients come from the same two hospitals.

## Curation notes

We start with all files from Kaggle.

The original data has one file per user that was already preprocessed to one .csv file by the competition creators. Here we start with the preprocessed single .csv file. The data is non-IID in nature based on the groups of patients from different hospitals. The data is grouped per patient ("Patient_ID"), and each patient's rows are the hours of one ICU stay. We simulate a snapshot early warning for patients not seen in training: at every hour, predict whether sepsis follows within the next 6 hours from that hour's measurements only. The model is trained once on the training patients and not refit with a new patient's data, and no prediction uses the patient's other hours. (The PhysioNet 2019 challenge also allowed the patient's earlier hours; causal history features built in `_clean` would be the upgrade to that setting.) One prediction per patient from the whole stay is not a valid alternative: all 2,932 septic records end at most 9 hours after their first positive hour, so the record length gives the outcome away.
In the original competition, one had to predict for unseen patients from the existing hospitals and also for a new hidden hospital. In the public data, we only have two hospitals, (A) and (B). Given the limited data, we decide not to simulate a domain shift as we could not "train" for domain shift. Thus, we simulate only a normal grouped non-IID scenario. That is, we use all patients from both hospitals for training and testing, but ensure that the splits are grouped by patient ID. Thus, we simulate what would happen if someone trains a model on data from two hospitals and uses this to predict for other patients from these hospitals. This decision is also amplified by the large gap in performance for the unseen hospital in the competition (Report, Table 3), clearly pointing to a domain shift that is out-of-scope for this task. Note, we do not have temporal information of the order of patients, thus, we cannot simulate to only predict for patients "from the future". We believe this does not introduce any data leakage for this dataset.

- Patients with an ID larger than 100_000 are from hospital (B), while patients with an ID smaller than 100_000 are from hospital (A). We add this indicator into our data and then reset the Patient_IDs to be continuous increasing integers.
- The data consists of a lot of missing values due to missing measurements.
- Note, hour is a reset time index per patient. ICULOS is similar, but with an offset. We keep both as this can show that we have truncated data for a patient.
- We reverse the ordinal encoding of Gender.
- We mark features as categorical where appropriate.
- For each patient, we predict the sepsis status over time. So it is more or less a transformed survival task (also in the original challenge).
- Prediction point and scoring: each row is one patient-hour with only that hour's measurements, and SepsisLabel is 1 from 6 hours before sepsis onset onward, so the task is "sepsis within the next 6 hours" at every hour. No row encodes the time left in the stay. The leak risk lies in the evaluation: the test set holds complete patient records, and septic records end shortly after onset, so a method that looks at a patient's later rows (record length, last Hour/ICULOS) learns the outcome. Each hour is predicted from its own row only: no feature may combine a patient's rows (record length, last Hour/ICULOS, aggregates over the stay). Scoring uses the PhysioNet 2019 utility per patient over its ordered hours, not independent rows.
- Patient_ID is the group column. We store it as a category, so the README's list of column types shows it
  (as it shows the target), but it is group metadata, not a model feature: the 42 features leave it out.
- The target (2026-11-01, CLEAN-25): `SepsisLabel` ships its classes as words (1 is the event). Binary: "Yes" is
  the event, the class that sorts second, so it is the metrics' positive class.
- The row budget (1M train, 500k test rows): We keep the whole frame (1,552,210 hours of 40,336 patients) and use grouped 3-fold cross-validation over it (v2 split protocol). Each fold tests on at most 500k rows, whole patients drawn from its third, and trains on every other row (about 1.05M). The train budget is recorded in the container, not applied: a benchmark sub-samples each train side to 1M rows with `subsample_train`, whose default draws whole patients at random. Both draws are stratified by each patient's most frequent label.
- Time basis: UnseenGroup (Andrej, 2026-11-18; `ForcedGroupedFromTemporal` before): the use case scores new ICU patients, whom the data does not hold, so whole patients are held out with all their hours. The source keeps only hours since admission, so the calendar order across patients is lost. Not tested: patients admitted later, which a dated release would allow.

## Group structure

One group is an ICU stay; its rows are its hourly records. The PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 predicts sepsis 6 hours ahead "at each hour of a patient's clinical record" for unseen patients and scores a utility per patient over its ordered hours. We use the snapshot form: each hour is one prediction, made from its own row only (no feature combines a patient's rows), and the challenge utility per patient is the score. The challenge also allowed the patient's earlier hours, a possible upgrade with causal history features.
