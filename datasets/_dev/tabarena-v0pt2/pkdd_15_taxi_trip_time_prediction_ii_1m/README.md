## In brief

**Question.** How long will a Porto taxi trip take, from its start to its destination?

**Use case.** For example, at pickup a taxi company estimates the trip's duration from the start, the destination and the time, to plan when the taxi will be free again.

**One row.** One trip of a Porto taxi fleet, July 2013 to June 2014 (ECML/PKDD 2015 challenge).

**Predicted.** The travel time in seconds, from the trip's GPS points (one every 15 seconds), on a log scale.

**Known at prediction time.** The local start time, the start point and the last GPS point as the destination, how the taxi was hired (dispatch call, taxi stand or street), the stand, and the taxi.

**What the test data stands for.** Each of April, May and June 2014 is a test month; training is a random million earlier trips, as for a model refit monthly.

**Our changes to the source.** The challenge asked for the remaining time of a trip under way; we predict the whole trip at pickup with its end point, and drop non-trips, GPS jumps, meters left running and the caller's phone number.

> **Use case fit: approximate (a stand-in with a known gap).** The challenge page says drivers do not enter the destination, so a destination known at pickup is our assumption, read from the trip's last GPS point. Without it the signal is weak (R² 0.11 to 0.15).

## Curation notes

We start with train.csv of the ECML/PKDD 2015 Discovery Challenge (UCI id 339): 1,710,670 Porto taxi trips from 2013-07-01 to 2014-06-30, each with its GPS trajectory (`POLYLINE`, one point every 15 s).

- Prediction point: the start of the trip, when the meter is switched on. The task is the travel time from origin and destination, an ETA task like `nyc_taxi_trip_duration` (the challenge's Task II starts from a partial trajectory).
- Anomaly: the challenge page says that drivers "do not indicate the final destination of the ride" to the dispatch system. Our destination is the last GPS point, so the task assumes it is entered at pickup, the curator's framing (record, CC 2026-09-21). Without it, LightGBM's R^2 is 0.11 to 0.15.
- Target: `log1p_travel_time`, log1p of the travel time in seconds, (GPS points - 1) x 15, the challenge's definition; RMSE on it is the challenge's metric, RMSLE.
- `_prepare_raw_files` parses each polyline into its number of points and its first and last point; the route is known only after the trip and is not a feature.
- `start_time`: TIMESTAMP is Unix time in UTC; the pickup profile by UTC hour shifts by one hour at both daylight-saving switches (decision table), so we convert it to Porto local time without a time zone.
- Rows dropped (counts in the decision table): under 2 GPS points; a TRIP_ID that occurs twice or more (we cannot tell which copy is right); a start outside Greater Porto (lat 41.0 to 41.3, lon -8.8 to -8.4); under 1 minute (a meter switched on and off without a ride) or over 3 hours (a meter left running); a straight line from start to end faster than 120 km/h (a GPS jump); and the last trips, which start on 1 July 2014 in local time. The 1-minute cut sits on a slope: 70% of 15-second trips end within 100 m of their start, 31% at 60 s, 7% at 120 s.
- We drop TRIP_ID (an id, the start second and the taxi), DAY_TYPE (constant "A"; the data page says it "has not been correctly calculated") and MISSING_DATA (erroneous per Lam et al. 2015, arXiv:1509.05257, and it describes the trajectory).
- We drop ORIGIN_CALL, the anonymised phone number of a dispatch-central customer: with the destination known it does not help LightGBM (R^2 -0.001 to +0.002).
- `call_type` A, B and C become dispatch_central, taxi_stand and street. `origin_stand` is empty unless the taxi was hired at a stand; we set the empty strings missing.
- `taxi_id` stays a categorical feature, since the taxi is known at pickup. The fleet is the same in every month (decision table), so the split is temporal and not grouped by taxi.
- Anomaly: we keep the 1.2% of trips that jump more than 1 km between two GPS points (missing points, Lam et al. 2015), trips with a drive back on the meter, and the 65,708 (4.0%) that end within 500 m of their start: no filter on the end point.

Version of `pkdd_15_taxi_trip_time_prediction_ii`: The cleaned frame holds 1,655,983 trips, above the 1.5M-row budget, and the train side of every window holds more than 1M. We sample per test window (v2 split protocol): each window keeps at most 500k of its rows (all of them here), and its train side is a random 1M of all earlier trips, drawn in one random order for all windows; the frame keeps only the rows a split uses.

## Splits

A model refit every month predicts the travel times of the next month's trips: 3 test windows of one calendar month (June, May and April 2014, newest first), and train is all earlier trips. Each window keeps all of its trips (131,659 to 156,990, under the 500k budget); each train side is a random 1M of all earlier trips (one random order for all windows). The challenge itself tested on 320 trips 1.5 to 6 months after its training year.

3 repeat(s) x 1 fold(s) with seed 4267; train 1,000,000 rows, test 131,659 to 156,990 rows. Prediction horizon: 1 months.

## Decisions

### 1. Rows dropped before the split

Trips without a travel time or a destination, duplicate ids, GPS errors and meter misuse go, and so do the last trips of the file, which start on 1 July in local time.

| rows dropped | dropped | left |
|---|---|---|
| fewer than 2 GPS points (no travel time or no destination) | 36510 | 1674160 |
| TRIP_ID (start second and taxi) more than once among the trips with 2 points or… | 21 | 1674139 |
| start outside Greater Porto | 937 | 1673202 |
| travel time under 60 s | 15533 | 1657669 |
| travel time over 3 h | 827 | 1656842 |
| straight line from start to end faster than 120 km/h | 741 | 1656101 |
| start on 2014-07-01 in Porto local time | 118 | 1655983 |

### 2. Temporal split; the taxi stays a feature, not a group

A v2 task is grouped or temporal, not both, so the verdict's 'temporal split, taxi as group' can only be one of them. The fleet is the same in every month (99.93% to 100% of each test month's trips are by taxis seen before), so a model in use predicts trips of taxis it has seen; a split by taxi would test new drivers, which deployment rarely meets.

| test month | trips | taxis | taxis new in this month | trips by a taxi seen before |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014-04 | 131659 | 432 | 1 | 99.93% |
| 2014-05 | 156990 | 434 | 1 | 100.00% |
| 2014-06 | 148236 | 430 | 0 | 100.00% |

### 3. Start time in Porto local time

TIMESTAMP is Unix time (UTC): the weekday pickup profile moves by one UTC hour at each daylight-saving switch and matches again once shifted, so hours of the day are only comparable in local time.

| switch | correlation, same UTC hour | correlation, shifted by one hour |
|---|---|---|
| 2013-10-27, summer to winter time | 0.92 | 0.992 |
| 2014-03-30, winter to summer time | 0.901 | 0.994 |
