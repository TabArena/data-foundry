- As published: 23 weekly tar.gz archives, each holding one headerless CSV: 17 calibration weeks (March week 3 to June week 4, 2016, path `download/normal/...`) and 6 test weeks (July week 5 to August week 5, path `download/attack/...`). The week list comes from the mirror's manifest (https://huggingface.co/datasets/mehedi107/ugr-16, `metadata/ugr16_manifest.json`), which gives sizes for 20 of the archives, 215.2 GB together; the card says 239 GB for all 23. I read 13 columns from 2-3 MB previews of `may_week1` and `august_week5`: flow end time (to the second), duration, source and destination IP, source and destination port, protocol, TCP flags, forwarding status, type of service, packets, bytes, label. The August week 5 CSV alone is 3,887,684,054 bytes uncompressed, about 40 million rows at the preview's 96.5 bytes per row. Total: "In total, the dataset contains 16.7 billion unidirectional flow records collected using NetFlow v9" (arXiv 2310.06656, Sect. 4.2). The scout's 16.6 billion and Lystea's "17B rows" are close to this. Labels: background, blacklist, anomaly-spam, anomaly-sshscan, anomaly-udpscan, dos, scan11, scan44, nerisbotnet. The official page (https://nesg.ugr.es/nesg-ugr16/) and the paper (ScienceDirect; the green copy at digibug.ugr.es) still refused this session.
- The task's table: the test part's classes, from Table 2 of the same group's dataset-integration paper (Magán-Carrión et al., arXiv 2112.02080, Sect. 3.1): background about 4,000M (97.14%), spam about 78M (1.96%), blacklist about 18M (0.46%), DoS about 9M (0.23%), port scan about 6M (0.14%), botnet about 2M (0.04%), UDP scan about 1M (0.03%), SSH scan 64 flows. That makes about 4.1 billion flows, about 118M of them not background (computed from the percentages). The calibration weeks hold about 12.6 billion flows (computed: 16.7 minus 4.1). No per-class counts for them were found; the two previews show only background and blacklist (May week 1: 205,317 and 372 rows; August week 5: 138,432 and 781). How the labels were made (arXiv 2310.06656, Sect. 4.2, reading the original paper): dos, scan11 and scan44 were launched by the authors (a low-rate SYN flood on port 80; one-to-one and four-to-four SYN scans). For nerisbotnet, real infections were not possible on the production network, so public Neris botnet traffic was converted to flows and inserted with rewritten timestamps and IP addresses (paraphrased). "Four public denylists ... were used to label flows corresponding to IP addresses" (blacklist; the same paper notes that such a flow need not contain an attack). "Labels anomaly-spam, anomaly-udpscan, and anomaly-sshscan were produced by using three different anomaly detectors", which the authors then confirmed by hand. "The rest of captured traffic from the ISP got the background tag." Inputs: 9 (duration, the two ports, protocol, flags, forwarding status, type of service, packets, bytes). The IPs are identifiers that carry the blacklist label and the injected hosts (LEAK), and the time serves the split. The group's own later work scores aggregates per time interval: 134 counter features per minute (FaaC), ROC AUC (Camacho and Rodríguez-Gómez, Data 10(3):33, 2025, arXiv 2305.19770, Sect. II). That is a host-window table we would have to build ourselves.
- Access: the official site answers 403 here. The Hugging Face mirror `mehedi107/ugr-16` serves the 23 weekly archives with SHA-256 sums, about 239 GB, no login and no licence stated. It leaves out the per-attack CSV subsets, the attack timestamp files and the nfcapd files, which the official site offers per week (https://fkie-cad.github.io/COMIDDS/content/datasets/ugr16). The 6 test weeks are 56.8 GB compressed (7.71 + 12.08 + 12.06 + 11.86 + 12.51 + 0.57 GB in the manifest), so a build streams about 4.1 billion rows once to keep the labelled ones and a sample of the rest. The per-attack subsets would make this cheap if the site answers. The paper's green copy is CC BY-NC-ND (Semantic Scholar); the data's terms were not found.
- Changes the call: (1) ctu_13 was rejected (`No`, `AHDS`, Lennart, 2026-11-04) because its labelled rows were staged infections against chosen hosts while the real traffic stayed an unlabelled Background. UGR'16's dos, scan11, scan44 and nerisbotnet classes share those facts: launched by the authors or spliced in, inside a background that is "the rest of captured traffic". The neris traffic is likely a capture like CTU-13's (scenarios 1, 2 and 9 are Neris), but no source here names it. What sets UGR'16 apart are the real anomaly classes found in customers' traffic: spam (about 78M test flows), SSH and UDP scans. Their labels come from the authors' detectors, the pattern of kyoto_2006_plus (`TBD -> 2nd Tier`, priority 1), and blacklist is an IP-list label. Camacho and Rodríguez-Gómez (2025) also report an unlabelled anomaly in the June calibration data and mislabelled test flows that "affects the reliability of the ROC/AUC" (Sect. III). (2) The gap point rested on the attack family as a multiclass target. Those families come from three label mechanisms (launched or spliced, detector, denylist), so the family target has no single real-world meaning. As a binary attack flag, the domain and the temporal regime are not thin. Proposed priority 2: gap 0, use case 0, source +, shape +, risk -. This is not a fast reject, because the real anomaly classes are not generated. A consistency question for the curator: does ctu_13's `AHDS` reading cover the launched and spliced classes, which would leave a spam-and-scan detector task on real ISP traffic?
