## Review

**Outcome:** added. The CoIL Challenge 2000 (The Insurance Company benchmark, UCI): 9,822 customers of a Dutch
insurer, 43 socio-demographic figures of the customer's postal area and the customer's holdings of 21 other insurance
products; the target is whether the customer holds a caravan (mobile home) policy (6%), scored as the challenge did,
by the share of policy holders among the 20% of customers a model ranks highest (the challenge's 800 of 4,000), on
random customers; the challenge's training and evaluation customers are pooled. The AI recommends shipping it, with the
reason for keeping the copied rows written down (issue 1). Reviewed without a review panel, from the gathered
evidence: the record, model runs on the shipped data and on the TabArena v0.1 version, the BeyondArena
results and checks on the shipped data. Validity: the definition keeps the challenge's task and its metric; holding a
policy stands in for interest in buying one (fit `approximate`); 27% of the rows sit in groups of identical rows, which
behave like neighbours with the same simple holdings, not like one customer entered twice (issue 1). Value: on nine
BeyondArena folds the methods spread from ROC AUC 0.704 (random forest) and 0.734 (linear model) to 0.786 (TabFM),
with stable ranks (Kendall's W 0.92); a hierarchical logistic model written by hand ranks first of 41
benchmark methods, so the task is close to a known additive structure. Quality: nothing else open. Weighed: a documented
benchmark with the challenge's own decision and metric +, a current-ownership label and natural copies -.

**Main points:** none open. The identical rows stay with the reason written down (issue 1, resolved 2026-11-11);
the random split is named a `Snapshot` (issue 2, resolved 2026-11-11).

**Record judgment:** valid. The use case is the challenge's: an insurer selects customers for a direct-mail offer of
caravan insurance (https://kdd.ics.uci.edu/databases/tic/tic.task.html); the metric was settled by Lennart on
2026-10-21. Andrej's early note ("might require split by customers") is answered by the data: each row is one
customer, and customers of one postal area share the 43 area columns, which new customers of the same areas share too,
so a random split of customers mirrors a mailing to the insurer's own book. A simple model (penalised
logistic regression on the car and fire premium bands and smoothed area profiles, blended with a small boosted model)
scores 1 - AUC 0.212 on the shipped splits against 0.214 for the best benchmark method: a value note, no reason to
retire.

2. **Time basis: name the random split a `Snapshot` of one extract of the insurer's book?**

   * **Problem:**
     - The label is whether a customer holds a caravan policy at one undated extract of the insurer's book (before March 2000), read together with the 21 other holdings and the postal-area figures, so every row is the same moment.
     - The record's collection fact fits (`Samples: One Moment`, no target tag: the label exists with the features), and the random split carries no time basis.
     - Name it a `Snapshot`, with the line below?
   * **Background:** a split that only fills in a moment the data covers is a `Snapshot`; its line names the moment,
     and no row of the same moment may carry a test row's target[^2t1]. The mailing selects, at the extract, the
     customers who look like holders: the same book at the same moment, and the challenge's 4,000 evaluation
     customers are another part of the same extract. Customers of one postal area share its 43 area columns, which a
     mailing to the same book sees too; no row gives another's policy away (issue 1's copies behave as different
     customers). What the split cannot test: who buys after the mailing.
   * **Effect on the task:** none on rows, splits or scores.
   * **Unknown:** whether some of the 21 other policies were bought together with the caravan policy, as a package,
     which would make them part of the holding rather than a sign of interest; the source does not say.
   * **Options:**
